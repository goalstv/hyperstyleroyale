Safety and compliance

Engineering and safety standards

Converting a classic car to electric introduces high-voltage systems capable of causing serious injury or death. These standards apply to every design we publish and every shop we certify.

High-voltage safety. EV conversions involve lethal DC voltages. Installation and commissioning must be performed or supervised by a qualified, HV-trained technician following our safety standards.

High-voltage work

  • Work on HV systems must be performed or directly supervised by a technician trained for high-voltage electric vehicle service.
  • Always isolate the pack, remove the service disconnect and verify zero voltage before working on HV components.
  • Use insulated tools and appropriately rated PPE. Never work on HV systems alone.
  • Commission every build with insulation-resistance and pre-charge tests, and keep the results in the build file.

Structural vs. non-structural parts

Every part in a Fitment Pack is labeled. Structural parts carry loads (motor mounts, adapter plates, battery boxes and their brackets) and must be produced by CNC machining, fabrication from the specified material, or certified metal printing. Non-structural parts (clips, bezels, harness guides) may be printed in-house in the specified polymer.

Design use terms

Before downloading structural designs you must confirm that parts will be produced with the specified materials and methods, installed by a qualified technician, and inspected according to the pack checklist. Validation badges

Engineer reviewed shows the date a qualified engineer last reviewed the pack revision. Field proven counts completed builds that reported back using that revision.

Certified Installer program

Shops earn the Certified Installer badge by documenting HV training for at least one technician, passing a commissioning audit on a completed build, and maintaining build files for every RESTOVOLT conversion. Level 2 adds a second trained technician and a dedicated commissioning area.

Titling, insurance and registration

Requirements for converted vehicles vary by state and country and change over time. In many places you will need to update the fuel type on the title or registration, may need an inspection, and must notify your insurer. Read our guide on how to register and insure a converted electric car, and always confirm local rules with your motor-vehicle agency and insurer.
