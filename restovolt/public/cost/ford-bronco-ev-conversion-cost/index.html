What drives the cost of a Bronco conversion

Conversion considerations

Our Bronco designs retain the factory-style transfer case so the truck keeps true four-wheel drive and low range. The Core Fitment Pack includes a motor mount, a motor-to-transmission adapter plate and an alignment collar that keep the output shaft on the original driveline angle, which matters more on a short-wheelbase vehicle where U-joint angles are already steep.

Rear drive-unit swaps are not supported on this platform yet because they would remove the rear axle and with it the four-wheel-drive layout most owners are paying for. Shops planning an on-road-only build should talk to us about a scan submission for a dedicated two-wheel-drive design.

Weight and packaging notes

There is less space in an early Bronco than its boxy shape suggests. The engine bay takes a modest box above the motor, and the second box sits in the rear footwell and former fuel-tank area with a skid-plate floor rated for trail impacts. The configurator caps current designs at 58 kWh to protect ground clearance and keep added weight within what lifted suspension and upgraded brakes can manage.

Every extra hundred pounds is felt on a 3,000 lb truck, so we recommend matching pack size to how the Bronco will really be used. A 40 kWh pack suits weekend trail rigs; 58 kWh suits owners who drive to the trailhead.

Line items people forget

  • Brake upgrades sized for the new weight and torque
  • Springs and dampers re-rated for the battery mass
  • DC-DC converter, 12 V battery relocation and gauge integration
  • Coolant loop, pump and radiator for the inverter and motor
  • Heating and defrost, if the car will be driven year-round
  • HV commissioning tests, labeling and the documentation file for insurance and registration

Bronco conversion cost FAQs

How much does a Bronco EV conversion cost?

Sample planning bands put complete drivetrain parts for a 1966–1977 Ford Bronco between $21,000 and $43,000, plus 230–360 hours of shop labor. Pack size and whether you keep the gearbox drive most of the difference.

What is the cheapest way to electrify a Bronco?

Keep the original transmission with an adapter plate, choose tested salvage battery modules sized for your real driving, and use the verified Bronco Core Fitment Pack rather than custom-designing mounts.

How much range will an electric Bronco have?

Typical builds see 60–150 miles at about 380 Wh per mile, depending on pack size, tires and speed.

Can an electric Bronco keep four-wheel drive?

Yes. RESTOVOLT Bronco designs keep a transfer case, so the truck retains 4WD and low range. The motor drives through an adapter plate and the original driveline geometry.
