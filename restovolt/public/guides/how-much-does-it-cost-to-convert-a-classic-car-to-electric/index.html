How Much Does It Cost to Convert a Classic Car to Electric?

Most professional classic car EV conversions in the US land between roughly $45,000 and $110,000 all-in. The spread is driven less by the car than by three decisions: how much battery you buy, whether you keep the gearbox, and how much of the car is restored at the same time.

By Sample Author, P.E. · Published March 4, 2026 · Engineer reviewed September 12, 2026

The four cost buckets

Every conversion quote can be broken into four buckets. Understanding them is the fastest way to compare quotes from different shops and to see where your money actually goes.

  • Drivetrain parts: motor, inverter, battery modules, BMS, charger, DC-DC converter, contactors, fusing and cabling. Usually 45–60% of the total.
  • Fabrication: motor mounts, adapter plates, battery boxes and brackets, either designed from scratch or produced from a Fitment Pack. Usually 5–15%.
  • Labor: teardown, fabrication, install, high-voltage commissioning and road testing. Usually 30–45%.
  • Supporting upgrades: brakes, springs, dampers, cooling and gauges. Often forgotten, typically $2,000–$8,000.

Batteries are the biggest single decision

Battery cost scales almost linearly with capacity, and so does weight. Salvage OEM modules that have been capacity- and isolation-tested are the most common choice for restomods, and a tested 40 kWh pack typically costs about half what the same capacity would cost in new, purpose-built modules.

The cheapest kilowatt-hour is the one you do not buy. Size the pack for how the car will be driven. A weekend roadster rarely needs more than 100 miles of range; a truck that tows or commutes may justify 60 kWh or more.

Reversible vs. permanent builds

Keeping the original transmission with an adapter plate is usually the less expensive route, because it reuses the existing driveline and requires no frame modification. It also keeps the conversion reversible, which protects the value of collectible cars.

Replacing the rear axle with a salvage OEM drive unit can lower parts cost per kilowatt but adds fabrication hours and commits the car to permanent changes. On trucks it can be worth it; on collectible coupes it rarely is.

Where Fitment Packs change the math

Designing mounts, adapter plates and battery boxes from scratch commonly consumes 40 to 80 hours of a shop’s time before the first part is cut. A verified, vehicle-specific design replaces that with a download and a dry fit, and because the same design has been fitted before, rework drops as well. That is the core reason RESTOVOLT exists.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to convert a classic car to electric?

Choose a light car, keep the original transmission, buy tested salvage battery modules sized for your real driving, and use a verified Fitment Pack instead of custom-designing mounts.

Does an EV conversion increase a classic car’s value?

It depends on the car and the market. Reversible, well-documented conversions of non-original cars tend to hold value best; converting rare, numbers-matching cars can reduce value to collectors.

How long does a professional conversion take?

Most shops quote 8 to 16 weeks of calendar time for 150 to 350 hours of labor, depending on platform, pack size and how much restoration is included.
