# RAP TRENDS — Investor Summary

**Working name:** RAP TRENDS · **Tagline:** Hip-Hop Is Happening Now.
**Stage:** Prototype complete. Pre-licensing, pre-distribution, pre-revenue.
**Seeking:** $5M to complete Phase 1 and enter Phase 2.

---

## The opportunity

Hip-hop is the most commercially significant genre in American music and has no dedicated,
editorially credible television network. The channels that built the format either left music
programming or lost the trust that made their countdowns matter. Nothing replaced them.

At the same time the economics inverted. Independent artists build careers without labels, and the
information they need — what is moving, who is breaking, how ownership actually works — is scattered
across social feeds, paywalled trade press, and platform dashboards that only show them their own
numbers.

There is a network-shaped hole and an intelligence-shaped hole, and they are the same hole.

## What we built

Seven products from one operation:

1. A 24/7 linear television channel
2. A FAST and connected-TV channel
3. An over-the-air network through partnerships with licensed stations
4. A digital and syndicated radio property
5. A website, mobile, and connected-TV experience
6. The RAP TRENDS Index — a real-time cultural intelligence platform
7. A promotional and distribution platform for artists

All programmed and originated through **RAP TRENDS OS**: newsroom, media library, rights engine,
programming calendar, channel origination, distribution control, and advertising, running as one
product.

## The defensible asset

Not the channel. Channels are replicable.

**The Index is the asset.** A published, auditable, multi-signal ranking model with time decay,
regional and emerging-artist adjustments, anomaly detection, confidence scoring, human override with
attribution, and a complete audit history. Fifteen signal types, each with a stated legal basis, each
weighted in public.

Two properties make it hard to copy:

**It is honest in a way that costs money.** A signal from a source we are not licensed to read
contributes nothing — it lowers the published confidence figure instead of being estimated. A
position without enough evidence is held off air rather than guessed at. Competitors who fill gaps
with estimates will look more confident and be less trustworthy, and in a business built on
credibility that difference compounds.

**Its integrity is enforced structurally.** Chart position is not for sale, and that is not a
promise in a policy document — the submission review queue reads a database view that omits the
plan, so an editor scoring a record cannot see whether the artist paid. No commercial role holds any
editorial permission, and the test suite asserts it.

Every chart that ever mattered eventually monetised its own positions and became worthless. Building
the firewall into the schema is the bet that not doing so is worth more.

## Business model

Seventeen revenue streams across FAST advertising, sponsorship, branded content, artist services,
memberships, events, licensing, commerce, data, production, and international channel licensing.

Standing monthly sponsorship positions carry an indicative rate-card ceiling near **$488K/month**.
Artist plans run free / $29 / $499. Memberships run $6 and $18.

Not assumed anywhere in the model: cable carriage fees, guaranteed platform revenue,
measurement-dependent CPMs, or archive revenue before clearance.

## What exists today

Built and verified: the domain layer (Index scoring, rights gate, schedule validation, ad safety,
editorial workflow) with 155 passing tests; the public network site; RAP TRENDS OS with role-based
access across twelve roles; artist and affiliate portals; and a public API including a standards-
compliant EPG.

Not built: persistence, an identity provider, the media pipeline, and playout — each an integration
behind an interface that already exists.

## Risks, stated plainly

| Risk | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Music licensing** | The largest cost and the hardest dependency. No software reduces it. Budgeted at ~$345K per six months in Phase 1 |
| **Distribution is not guaranteed** | FAST, cable, and OTA all require agreements that do not exist. OTA additionally requires a licensed station partner — we hold no spectrum and no FCC licence |
| **Original production is expensive** | It is also the differentiator. A cheaper network is a video jukebox, and jukeboxes do not get carriage |
| **Audience is unproven** | Phase 1 exists to prove it before more capital is committed |
| **Regulatory** | Broadcast counsel and music-licensing professionals must approve the operating model before anything transmits |

## Why this team, this product

Most media startups pitch a channel and build a website. This one built the operating system first —
the rights engine that refuses to air uncleared content, the scheduler that catches dead air before
it happens, the ad gate that will not traffic cannabis onto a broadcast feed, and a chart whose
method is published. Those are the systems that make a media company insurable, carriable, and
trustworthy, and they are the ones nobody builds until a lawsuit or a takedown forces them to.

The programming is the product. The operating system is why the programming can be sold to a cable
operator, a station group, and an advertiser without a compliance conversation ending the deal.
