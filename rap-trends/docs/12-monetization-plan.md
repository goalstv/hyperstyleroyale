## 3. Advertising

**Inventory.** Eight minutes per hour on the linear channel, structured as programme, break, promo,
break so the load is predictable. Eight to twelve minutes per hour reserved for affiliate insertion
on carriage deals.

**Products.** National advertising, local affiliate avails, programme sponsorships, branded content,
artist and concert promotion, shoppable television with QR codes and interactive overlays, dynamic
ad insertion, and contextual and geographic targeting.

**Technical.** SCTE-35 markers, VAST 4.2, VMAP, server-side insertion readiness, frequency capping,
and per-campaign content exclusions.

**Safety.** Restricted categories carry rules enforced in software before a spot can be trafficked —
by platform, daypart, territory, and age gate — and a licensor's own restrictions on a specific
programme are a second, independent gate. Cannabis cannot run on any broadcast or MVPD feed
regardless of state legality. Political inventory is not accepted on a broadcast feed until counsel
has approved the policy and the political file is in place.

## 4. Artist plans

| Plan | Price | Review window | What it buys |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 14 days | 2 submissions/month, verified profile, human review, full eligibility |
| Professional | $29/mo | 5 business days | Unlimited submissions, EPK and rights management, market-level reporting, priority for invitations |
| Enterprise | $499/mo | 3 business days | Roster accounts, bulk ingestion, API access, dedicated rights support, quarterly insight report |

**The firewall, stated on every plan and enforced in the schema.** A plan buys review speed,
workflow, and reporting detail. It buys no editorial outcome. The review queue reads a view that
omits the plan, so an editor scoring a record cannot see whether the artist paid.

Promotional products — release promotion $1,200, tour promotion $800, video premiere slot $2,400 —
are sold, labelled as promotion everywhere they appear, and separated from editorial and the chart.

## 5. Membership

| Tier | Price | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Free account | $0 | Voting, history, follows, notifications |
| Member | $6/mo | Ad-free on-demand on owned platforms, early SESSIONS access, the weekly Index briefing, awards ballot, ticket priority |
| Insider | $18/mo | Quarterly cultural-insight report, chart data exports, personal API access, archive access as clearance completes, taping invitations |

A member's vote counts exactly the same as a free account holder's. Selling weighted votes would
destroy the only thing the chart has.

## 6. Affiliate economics

All three packages are barter in year one — inventory split, no cash licence fee.

| Package | Local avails/hr | Network retains |
|---|---|---|
| Full-Time Carriage | 8 | 4 minutes/hour national |
| Daypart Block | 10 | 2 minutes/hour national |
| Program Syndication | 12 | 1 unit per quarter-hour |

Cash licence fees are a Phase 5 conversation, after there is an audience to point at.

## 8. What the model does not assume

- **No cable carriage fees.** They may never materialise for a channel at this stage.
- **No platform guarantees.** FAST revenue depends on agreements that do not exist.
- **No measurement-dependent CPMs.** Nielsen is bought when carriage justifies it, not before.
- **No revenue from the archive** until chain-of-title clearance completes.
- **No music licensing savings.** The licences are a cost of doing business and are budgeted as one.

## 9. The rule that governs all of it

Every commercial product on this list is designed so that it cannot influence editorial or the
chart. That constraint costs revenue — a network that sells chart positions makes more money in year
one. It is also the only reason anyone would trust the chart in year three, which is where the
business actually is.
