# RAP TRENDS — Affiliate Acquisition Strategy

## 1. What we are actually selling

To a station, RAP TRENDS is programming they cannot produce and cannot buy elsewhere, delivered
clean, compliant, and free of cash cost in year one, with inventory they keep.

That is the whole pitch. Everything below is about who to say it to, in what order, and with what
evidence in hand.

## 2. Why barter first

A new network asking a station for a cash licence fee is asking to be turned down, because the
station is being asked to pay for an audience that has not been demonstrated. Barter inverts the
risk: the station gives airtime it is not monetising well and keeps 8–12 minutes an hour to sell
locally. If the programming does not perform, they have lost inventory that was underperforming
anyway.

Cash licence fees are a Phase 5 conversation, after there is measured audience to point at.

## 3. Sequence

### Radio first (Phase 3)

Radio is the right first move for three reasons. The decision-maker is a programme director, not a
general manager. The programming drops into an existing clock without disturbing the format. And the
unit of commitment is small — a station can take a 60-second hourly update and see how it plays
before considering the two-hour weekend countdown.

**Target profile.** Urban contemporary, rhythmic CHR, and hip-hop-leaning stations in markets 10–75.
Independent and mid-sized groups before the largest ones — they move faster and their programme
directors have real autonomy.

**Opening offer.** The 60-second RAP TRENDS Update, hourly, barter, cancellable at 30 days. It costs
the station almost nothing to try, and the network gets airplay data, an affidavit relationship, and
a reference.

**Escalation.** Update → 5-minute report → NEXT UP Spotlight → daily countdown → weekend countdown →
full-time carriage.

### Television second (Phase 4)

**Target profile.** Independent stations and small-group owners with underused subchannels in
Atlanta, New York, Los Angeles, Miami, and Houston. Diginet operators looking for a demographic they
do not currently reach.

**Opening offer.** A fixed daypart block — afternoon drive or late night — with market exclusivity,
barter terms, 10 minutes an hour of local availability, and market-specific CITY REPORT content.

**Escalation.** Daypart block → extended daypart → full-time subchannel → multi-market group deal.

### Groups third (Phase 5)

Once individual stations can be pointed at, the conversation moves to group heads, and the unit
becomes a multi-market agreement.

## 5. What we ask in return

- Carriage of network identification and the top-of-hour clock
- Captions passed through without re-encoding
- Programmes aired intact, including sponsor billboards
- Adherence to the network's restricted-category advertising rules on inserted inventory
- Monthly or weekly reporting through the affiliate portal
- The network's retained national inventory

## 6. The objections, and honest answers

**"Who watches this?"**
In Phase 3, nobody on your air yet. Here is the FAST audience, the site traffic, and the radio
performance in the markets already carrying it. If that is not enough, take the 60-second update and
decide in 30 days.

**"What is the indecency risk?"**
Zero from our end. You receive the clean feed exclusively, and the origination system will not route
an explicit asset to a broadcast or radio-affiliate destination — the rights gate treats a missing
clean version as a hard blocker.

**"Do you have the music rights?"**
For everything on the feed, yes, and we will show you the rights records. Where we do not, the
content does not air — which is why our archive channel has not launched.

**"What happens to my local sales?"**
You keep 8–12 minutes an hour and sell them. We retain a small national position. On radio
syndication we retain one unit per quarter-hour and nothing else.

**"Why should I believe your chart?"**
Read the methodology page. Every weight is published, every source's licensing basis is published,
every editorial override is published with a name and a reason, and positions we do not have enough
evidence for are held off air rather than guessed at.

**"What if you fold?"**
Barter means you have risked inventory, not cash, and the agreement is cancellable. That is the
honest answer, and giving it is worth more than a confident one.

## 10. What this strategy cannot do

It cannot manufacture an audience, secure spectrum, or substitute for a licensed station partner in
the OTA case. Affiliate acquisition is a sales function operating on evidence the rest of the
business has to produce first, and no amount of process discipline changes that order.
